Explore Málaga

Málaga is a city where you can do a little bit of everything. You can visit old castles and museums, relax on sunny beaches, walk in the mountains and try tasty local food, all in just a few days. On this page you will find the best places to visit, split into four groups: must-see places, beaches, history and culture, and food. For each place you can read a short description, check the opening hours and click on the address to open it in Google Maps.

Must see places in Málaga

  1. Picasso Museum Málaga

    Any art lover will tell you a visit to Málaga isn’t complete without paying homage to the city’s most famous son, Pablo Picasso. The Picasso Museum is dedicated to the renowned painter, and the Málaga collection has the added flavor of being housed in a 16th-century Andalusian palace that blends Moorish and Renaissance architecture. Discover one masterpiece after another, such as “The Three Dancers,” “The Weeping Woman,” and “The Tragedy,” as you wander the exhibits showcasing collections from Picasso’s blue, cubist, and surrealist periods.

    Address

    Palacio de Buenavista, C. San Agustín, 8, Distrito Centro, 29015 Málaga

    Opening hours

    Daily 10:00–18:00

  2. Montes de Málaga Natural Park

    For some fresh air, drive 25 minutes north of the city to Montes de Málaga Natural Park. Nature lovers will appreciate the mix of rugged terrain and open spaces, where you can hike to scenic viewpoints, mountain bike on the trails, or plan a picnic. The 18-square-mile park is one of the top places in Málaga for escaping the city traffic and summer heat.

    Address

    Casabermeja-Colmenar, Málaga

    Opening hours

    Open 24 hours

  3. El Caminito del Rey

    The dizzying heights at El Caminito del Rey will test the nerves of serious thrill seekers. The “world’s most dangerous walkway” may look rickety, but it’s actually quite safe. While the adrenaline-pumping, heart-pounding experience isn’t for everyone, those who brave the 5-mile cliffside trail will be treated to spectacular views and an utterly unique experience that will prove why Málaga and its surroundings are one of the most beautiful places in Spain.

    Address

    El Caminito del Rey, 29200 Málaga (South Access)

    Opening hours

    Daily from 9:00. Closing hours vary by season

Beaches in Málaga

  1. Malagueta Beach

    A short walk from the historic center will bring you to Málaga’s liveliest beach. During summer, Malagueta Beach becomes a hub of activity, with warm Mediterranean waters, clear blue skies, and smooth, golden sand. Renting lounge chairs and umbrellas along the mile-long stretch is a fantastic way to soak up the sun, enjoy people-watching, and immerse yourself in the vibrant atmosphere! Pair your beach trip with a boat ride, sailing, or snorkeling for a more active day.

    Address

    Marítimo Pablo Ruiz Picasso, Málaga-Este, 29016 Málaga

    Opening hours

    Open 24 hours

  2. Playa Peñón del Cuervo

    The relaxed vibe, gentle clear water and quality picnic areas with bbqs. Motivated walkers and cyclists can also enjoy the well-kept coastal path which connects Raven Rock to Malagueta and other Malaga Beaches. Less crowds, picnic spots and free parking near the beach makes Raven Rock a campervan friendly choice.

    Address

    Playa Peñón del Cuervo

    Opening hours

    Open 24 hours

History & culture in Málaga

  1. Alcazaba of Málaga

    Start your cultural tour at the Alcazaba of Málaga, one of the best-preserved Moorish fortresses in the country. The Alcazaba was built to defend the city, and walking through the intricate archways and fortified walls brings Andalusia’s rich Islamic history to life. You’ll also find charming courtyards and patios with lush gardens, bubbling fountains, horseshoe arches, and intricate tilework.

    Address

    Alcazabilla, 2, Distrito Centro, 29012 Málaga

    Opening hours

    Daily 9:00–20:00

  2. Gibralfaro Castle

    A quarter mile up from the Alcazaba is the impressive Gibralfaro Castle. Join a tour to see the defensive walls, guard towers, cannons, ramparts, and medieval weapons with a history dating back to 1340. Beyond its rich history, the breathtaking panoramic views from the castle make it one of Malaga’s top attractions.

    Address

    Cam. Gibralfaro, 11, Distrito Centro, 29016 Málaga

    Opening hours

    Daily 9:00-18:00

  3. Málaga Cathedral

    You’ll notice one of the towers of the Málaga Cathedral is missing, but it’s no less a masterpiece of architecture thanks to its grand façade, elaborate carvings, and soaring columns. Inside, you’ll see vaulted ceilings, 18th-century frescoes, and some of Spain’s finest craftsmanship in the cathedral’s mahogany and cedar choir stalls. Don’t forget to climb to the rooftop terrace for one of the best views of Málaga.

    Address

    C. Molina Lario, 9, Distrito Centro, 29015 Málaga

    Opening hours

    Monday-Saturday 10:00-18:00, Sunday 14:00-18:00

Food in Málaga

  1. Gazpachuelo

    Unlike its more famous cousin, gazpacho, gazpachuelo is a warm fish soup unique to Málaga. This comforting dish combines potatoes, white fish, and a mayonnaise-based broth. Originally a fisherman’s meal born from necessity, it’s now served in many of the city’s finest restaurants.

    Where to eat

    Mesón Mariano Chiringuito Marina Playa

  2. Ajo blanco

    Often called “white gazpacho,” ajo blanco is a cold soup made from ground almonds, garlic, bread, and olive oil that dates back to Roman times. The soup’s creamy texture and subtle flavor make it a favorite among locals and visitors alike, particularly during Málaga’s sweltering summers.

    Where to eat

    Uvedoble Taberna La Tasquita de en Medio

  3. Berenjenas con miel

    These crispy fried eggplant slices drizzled with honey are a perfect example of Málaga’s Moorish heritage and the lasting influence of Arabic cuisine on the region. The eggplant slices are fried until golden and crispy on the outside and finished with a drizzle of honey or miel de caña (sugarcane molasses).

    Where to eat

    Bodega El Pimpi Casa Lola
